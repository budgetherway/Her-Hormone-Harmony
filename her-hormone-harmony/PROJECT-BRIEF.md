## 2. Vision & Objective

Her Hormone Harmony is a women's wellness content brand built around **cycle syncing** — aligning food, exercise, and lifestyle to the four phases of the menstrual cycle. The blog's job is to capture high-intent Pinterest/Google search traffic from women looking for hormone, PCOS, cortisol, and cycle-related solutions, and route them to the free guide and the paid Handbook, both fulfilled directly through Gumroad.

The blog is not a general wellness journal — every article exists to (1) rank/pin for a specific keyword, (2) solve a specific pain point, and (3) route the reader toward one of the two funnel assets.

## 4. Reader Persona

Drawn from the pain points already mapped in the keyword sheet, the core reader is a woman who:
- Has heard of "cycle syncing" but doesn't know how to apply it practically, or has tried it and seen no results
- Is dealing with one or more of: low energy, hormonal acne, bloating, PCOS, post-birth-control symptoms, high cortisol/stress, or hormonal weight gain
- Is too busy for complicated protocols and wants simple, phase-specific, done-for-her guidance
- Searches on Pinterest/Google with intent-heavy phrases ("follicular phase diet," "PCOS weight loss," "luteal phase workout")
- Is receptive to natural/food-based solutions over medication

## 6. Core Site Pages

Beyond the 300-article content library, four foundational pages are needed for the site to look credible, meet legal requirements around email capture and monetization, and give readers a way to reach out.

| Page | Purpose | Key elements |
|---|---|---|
| **About Us** | Builds trust and connection with the reader; explains who's behind Her Hormone Harmony and why | Founder story/mission framed around cycle syncing, brand voice consistent with Section 3, a natural mention of the Handbook |
| **Privacy Policy** | Legal requirement for a site collecting emails (the lead-magnet opt-in) or using analytics/ad tracking | What data is collected (email, cookies, analytics), how it's used, third-party tools involved (email service provider, Pinterest tag, Google Analytics), reader rights, contact for privacy requests |
| **Affiliate Disclosure** | Legal (FTC-style) requirement if any article links to affiliate products — supplements, kitchen tools, tracking apps — alongside the owned Handbook promotion | Plain-language disclosure that some links earn a commission at no extra cost to the reader; linked in the site footer and referenced at the top of any article containing affiliate links |
| **Contact** | Gives readers, brand partners, and press a way to reach the site | Contact form or email address, expected response time, an optional line for collaboration/partnership inquiries |

Privacy Policy and Affiliate Disclosure should be live before the email opt-in funnel or any affiliate links go public — About Us and Contact can follow shortly after but should still launch alongside the first batch of articles.

## 7. Article Production Framework

Most categories are built from a repeating **5-post cluster pattern** around one sub-topic (e.g. "follicular phase diet"):

1. **Pillar post** — the core how-to/what-to-eat article (unique CTA — e.g. "Download the Follicular Grocery Matrix in the eBook")
2. **"Ultimate Guide"** — deeper, more comprehensive version of #1
3. **"Common Mistakes"** — troubleshooting angle for women who've tried and failed
4. **"For Busy Schedules"** — simplified/low-effort angle
5. **"Science-Backed"** — credibility angle, citing the *why*

Every article, regardless of category, should follow this internal template:
- **H1** = the exact title from the sheet (already Pinterest/SEO-optimized — don't rewrite it)
- **Hook intro** (2–3 sentences) that names the reader's exact pain point from the "Target Pain Point" column
- **Body** (600–1,000 words): practical, phase-specific guidance — food lists, sample routines, explanations — genuinely useful on its own, not just a teaser
- **Mid-article content upgrade box** — soft opt-in for the free *7-Day Hormone Balancing Food Guide*
- **Closing CTA** — the specific hook from the "In-Article Product CTA Angle" column, pointing to the paid Handbook
- **Internal links** — to 2–3 related articles in the same phase/category to build topic clusters

## 8. Monetization Funnel

```
Pinterest pin (keyword-matched title)
        ↓
Blog article (solves the searched problem)
        ↓
Mid-article link → 7-Day Hormone Balancing Food Guide (free, direct to Gumroad)
        ↓
Closing CTA → The Complete Cycle Syncing Handbook (Gumroad)
```

No email capture or nurture sequence sits in this flow right now — both assets link straight to their Gumroad pages. An email layer can be added later without changing the articles themselves, since the CTA placement (mid-article + closing) stays the same either way.

**Free lead magnet:** *7-Day Hormone Balancing Food Guide* — positioned as the "quick win," offered once per article, framed around whatever the article's specific angle already is (never a generic "join my list" ask).

**Paid product:** *The Complete Cycle Syncing Handbook: A Phase-by-Phase Guide to Balancing Your Hormones, Energy, and Cravings Naturally* — every article's closing CTA should map to the specific bonus/chapter named in that row's CTA column (e.g. "the Complete Beginner's Cycle Guide," "the PCOS Healing Roadmap," "the Cortisol Reset Protocol"), so the promotion feels like a relevant next step rather than a repeated ad.

---

## 9. In-Article Promotion Guidelines

- **One** free-guide opt-in per article, placed after the reader has received real value (never above the fold)
- **One to two** paid-ebook mentions per article — one contextual mid-body mention (optional) and one clear closing CTA
- CTA copy should stay benefit-led and specific ("Get the full Phase Workout Schedule inside the Handbook") rather than generic ("Buy my ebook")
- No hard-sell language, pop-ups, or repeated asks within a single post — the funnel works on volume of articles, not pressure per article
