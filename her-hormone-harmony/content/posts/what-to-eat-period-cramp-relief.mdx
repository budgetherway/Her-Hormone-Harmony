If ibuprofen is doing most of the work through your period, food can help carry some of that load too. Cramps come largely from prostaglandins, compounds that trigger uterine contractions, and what you eat directly affects how many of them your body produces.

This isn't about willpower or eating clean. It's about a handful of specific foods that either feed inflammation or calm it down.

## What makes cramps worse

Excess omega-6 fats, the kind found in fried food, packaged snacks, and most vegetable oils, get converted into more prostaglandins. So does high sugar intake, which spikes inflammation in the same twenty-four hours you're already dealing with cramps and mood swings. Neither one is the villain of your whole diet, but both matter more this week than any other.

## What actually calms things down

- Omega-3s. Salmon, walnuts, and chia seeds compete with omega-6s for the same enzyme pathway, which means more omega-3 intake genuinely means fewer inflammatory prostaglandins produced.
- Magnesium. Dark chocolate, spinach, and pumpkin seeds help your uterine muscles relax instead of gripping through each contraction.
- Iron-rich food. Red meat, lentils, and dark leafy greens replace what you're losing through bleeding, especially if your flow runs heavy. Low iron here shows up as dizziness and a bone-deep fatigue that feels worse than normal tired.
- Ginger tea. Genuinely backed by research for period pain, not just a folk remedy. A cup or two a day during the worst of it does real work.

## A day that actually supports this

Breakfast: oatmeal with walnuts and a spoon of ground flaxseed. Lunch: lentil soup with dark leafy greens. Dinner: salmon with roasted vegetables. Ginger tea between meals if cramps are bad.

## Two habits that pair well with this

A warm bath or heating pad relaxes uterine muscle the same way magnesium does, just from the outside. And cutting caffeine to one cup this week, if you can manage it, helps too, since it constricts blood vessels and can intensify cramping for some women.

## When food isn't enough

Some cramps are just going to need a heating pad and ibuprofen, and that's fine. But if your periods are consistently debilitating, missed-work-and-can't-get-off-the-floor level, that's worth a real conversation with a doctor. Food helps. It's not a replacement for checking whether something like endometriosis is behind pain this severe.
