Researching magnesium forms, figuring out the right dose, and remembering to take a supplement consistently every day is more decision-making than a lot of weeks have room for. Here's the version that removes most of that.

## The one supplement worth buying

Look for magnesium glycinate specifically, in a dose around 200 to 300 milligrams, and take one capsule in the evening. This single choice avoids the more common, poorly absorbed oxide form without requiring you to research the differences between forms yourself.

## The one habit that makes this actually happen

Keep the bottle next to your toothbrush, so taking it becomes part of a routine you already do every night without fail. This removes the need for a separate reminder or app to track it.

## The easiest food addition, if you want one more thing

A small handful of pumpkin seeds or almonds as an afternoon snack adds meaningful magnesium without requiring a recipe or meal plan built around it. This is optional on top of the supplement, not a replacement for it.

## What to skip for now

Detailed dosing research, comparing every magnesium form, and tracking symptoms against intake precisely can all wait. One consistent evening capsule covers the core benefit without requiring more effort than that.

## What if you're not sure this is helping

Give it a full two cycles before deciding, since magnesium's effects build gradually rather than showing up immediately. If symptoms haven't improved at all after two consistent cycles, that's a reasonable point to consider a slightly higher dose or add the food source too, rather than assuming magnesium isn't for you after just a few days.

## What to do if capsules upset your stomach

Try taking it with a small amount of food rather than on an empty stomach, which resolves mild digestive discomfort for many people without needing to switch products or give up on the habit entirely. If discomfort continues, a lower dose split across two smaller servings during the day is a reasonable next adjustment.

## What this simplified version can and can't do

This captures the core benefit without requiring research or a complicated routine. If PMS symptoms remain severe despite this simple addition, building out more support, food sources, addressing stress, or a doctor's input, makes sense once you have more bandwidth to take it further.
