Chronic low-grade inflammation worsens insulin resistance, which in turn affects nearly every hormone this whole site talks about, from androgens to cortisol to estrogen metabolism. These five recipes lean on the foods with the most consistent research behind reducing that inflammation, without needing to eliminate entire food groups to get there.

## Turmeric Ginger Salmon

Turmeric and ginger both have well-documented anti-inflammatory compounds, paired here with salmon's omega-3s for a meal that hits this angle from multiple directions at once.

**Ingredients:** 2 salmon fillets, 1 tsp turmeric, 1 tbsp fresh ginger (grated), 2 tbsp olive oil, lemon juice, salt.

**Instructions:** Mix turmeric, ginger, olive oil, and salt into a paste, rub over salmon. Bake at 400°F for 12 to 15 minutes, finish with lemon juice.

## Berry and Walnut Spinach Salad

Berries provide antioxidants linked to reduced inflammatory markers, and walnuts add omega-3s in a plant-based form alongside a good dose of magnesium.

**Ingredients:** 4 cups spinach, 1 cup mixed berries, 1/4 cup walnuts, 1/4 cup crumbled goat cheese, olive oil, balsamic vinegar.

**Instructions:** Toss spinach with berries, walnuts, and goat cheese. Drizzle with olive oil and balsamic vinegar before serving.

## Golden Turmeric Lentil Soup

A warming, fiber-rich soup that combines turmeric's anti-inflammatory properties with lentils' fiber, which supports the gut health tied to overall inflammation levels.

**Ingredients:** 1 cup red lentils, 1 tbsp turmeric, 1 can coconut milk, 4 cups vegetable broth, 1 onion, garlic, ginger.

**Instructions:** Sauté onion, garlic, and ginger, add turmeric, cook 1 minute. Add lentils and broth, simmer 20 minutes, stir in coconut milk before serving.

## Olive Oil and Herb Roasted Vegetables

A simple side built around extra virgin olive oil, which contains oleocanthal, a compound with anti-inflammatory properties comparable in some research to mild anti-inflammatory medication.

**Ingredients:** 4 cups mixed vegetables (zucchini, bell pepper, red onion, cherry tomatoes), 3 tbsp extra virgin olive oil, fresh rosemary and thyme, salt.

**Instructions:** Toss vegetables generously with olive oil, herbs, and salt. Roast at 425°F for 25 to 30 minutes until tender and lightly charred.

## Chia and Flaxseed Overnight Oats

Chia and flax both provide omega-3s and fiber in a convenient make-ahead format, useful for a busy morning that still needs to support this goal.

**Ingredients:** 1/2 cup oats, 1 tbsp chia seeds, 1 tbsp ground flaxseed, 1 cup milk of choice, 1/2 cup berries, honey to taste.

**Instructions:** Combine oats, chia, flax, and milk in a jar, refrigerate overnight. Top with berries and honey before eating.

## Making these work for your kitchen

Any fatty fish works in place of salmon, and canned lentils speed up the soup considerably if time is tight. A dairy-free cheese or simply omitting it keeps the berry salad working well for anyone avoiding dairy.

## Making this a consistent pattern, not an occasional meal

The soup and roasted vegetables both keep well for several days, making a larger batch of either a practical way to have an anti-inflammatory option available most of the week rather than only on days there's time to cook from scratch. The overnight oats take under five minutes to prepare the night before and remove the temptation to skip breakfast entirely on a rushed morning.

## Why these recipes work together

Turmeric, ginger, omega-3-rich foods, and extra virgin olive oil all have research specifically behind reducing inflammatory markers, not just a general "healthy eating" reputation. Using them consistently, not just occasionally, is what actually moves the needle on the inflammation that quietly affects hormone regulation throughout your whole cycle.
