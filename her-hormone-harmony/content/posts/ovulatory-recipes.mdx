The few days around ovulation tend to bring peak energy, better mood, and a body that can handle more intensity, both in workouts and in what you're asking your digestion to process. These five recipes lean into that window, a little more vibrant, a little more effort, matching the energy you actually have during these days.

Since this window is short, usually just a handful of days each cycle, these recipes are also good candidates for a slightly more social meal, cooking for others or trying something a bit more involved than a weeknight default.

## Grilled Shrimp and Citrus Avocado Salad

Shrimp provides zinc, which supports healthy ovulation, paired with citrus for a bright, vitamin C-rich dressing that helps with iron absorption from other meals that week.

**Ingredients:** 1 lb shrimp, 2 cups mixed greens, 1 avocado (sliced), 1 orange (segmented), 2 tbsp olive oil, 1 tbsp lime juice, salt, pepper, chili flakes.

**Instructions:** Season shrimp with salt, pepper, and chili flakes, grill or pan-sear 2 minutes per side. Toss greens with olive oil and lime juice, top with avocado, orange segments, and shrimp.

## Seared Steak with Chimichurri and Roasted Potatoes

Red meat provides iron and zinc in a highly absorbable form, useful during a phase where your body can handle a heartier meal than it might a week later.

**Ingredients:** 1 lb sirloin steak, 2 cups baby potatoes, 1 cup parsley, 2 cloves garlic, 1/4 cup olive oil, 2 tbsp red wine vinegar.

**Instructions:** Roast potatoes at 425°F for 25 minutes. Blend parsley, garlic, olive oil, and vinegar for the chimichurri. Sear steak 4 minutes per side for medium, rest 5 minutes, slice, and top with chimichurri.

## Spicy Salmon Poke Bowl

Salmon and edamame both provide protein and healthy fats that support hormone production during this higher-performance window.

**Ingredients:** 8 oz sushi-grade salmon (cubed), 1 cup cooked rice, 1/2 cup edamame, 1/2 cucumber (sliced), 2 tbsp soy sauce, 1 tsp sesame oil, sriracha to taste.

**Instructions:** Mix salmon with soy sauce, sesame oil, and sriracha, let marinate 10 minutes. Serve over rice with edamame and cucumber.

## Roasted Vegetable and Chickpea Power Bowl

A dense, colorful bowl built for a day with more activity than usual, combining fiber, protein, and complex carbohydrates in one meal.

**Ingredients:** 1 can chickpeas, 2 cups mixed roasted vegetables (bell pepper, zucchini, red onion), 1 cup cooked farro, 2 tbsp tahini, lemon juice.

**Instructions:** Roast vegetables and chickpeas together at 400°F for 25 minutes with olive oil and salt. Serve over farro, drizzle with tahini thinned with lemon juice and water.

## Dark Chocolate and Berry Energy Bites

A quick, no-bake snack for a day with more going on than usual, providing magnesium and antioxidants without needing time in the kitchen.

**Ingredients:** 1 cup dates (pitted), 1/2 cup almonds, 2 tbsp cocoa powder, 1/4 cup dried berries, pinch of salt.

**Instructions:** Blend all ingredients in a food processor until a sticky dough forms. Roll into small balls, refrigerate at least 30 minutes before eating.

## Making these work for your kitchen

Chicken thighs swap in cleanly for shrimp or steak if that's what's available, and tofu marinated the same way as the shrimp works well for a plant-based version of the salad. Any firm white fish can replace salmon in the poke bowl.

## How to use these across a week

Since this window is short, prioritize the recipes that use ingredients you already have on hand rather than planning a big shopping trip specifically for a few days. The energy bites and roasted vegetable bowl both hold up well in the fridge for several days, making them useful to prep in a larger batch if you want something ready to reach for once this higher-energy window has passed.

## Why these recipes work together

This window can handle more protein, more iron-rich food, and slightly larger portions than the luteal or menstrual phases ahead, since energy and digestive capacity are both naturally higher right now. Using this short window well sets up more even energy for the harder phase that follows it.
