A full restorative yoga sequence with props, breathing techniques, and multiple poses is genuinely helpful, and also more than a lot of period days have energy for. Here's the version that fits into five minutes, right on your bed if getting to a mat feels like too much.

## The one pose worth doing if you only do one

Child's pose, forehead resting on the floor or a pillow, arms extended or resting by your sides, held for a minute or two while breathing slowly. This single position addresses lower back tension and provides a grounding, calming effect without requiring any other movement.

## A simple two-minute add-on

Roll onto your back, bring the soles of your feet together, and let your knees fall open, resting your hands on your belly. Stay here for another minute or two, breathing slowly, letting the position passively stretch your inner thighs and pelvic floor.

## Combining this with heat for more relief

Place a heating pad on your lower belly while in either position, combining two pain-relief approaches without requiring any additional time or effort. This is one of the easiest ways to get more benefit from the same five minutes.

## What to skip for now

A full sequence, specific breathing techniques, and props like blocks or bolsters can all wait. Two simple positions, held gently for a couple of minutes each, capture the core benefit without asking for more from a day you already have very little energy for.

## What if even five minutes feels like too much

Simply lying still with a heating pad, without any specific positioning, still provides real pain relief through heat alone, and that's a completely reasonable choice on a day movement genuinely isn't happening. This isn't a failure to do the "real" version, it's matching effort to what that specific day allows.

## What to do if you're somewhere without privacy

Seated forward fold, done right in a chair by leaning forward with your chest toward your knees, provides a similar lower back release to child's pose without needing to get on the floor, making it a discreet option for an office or anywhere else you can't easily lie down.

## What this simplified version can and can't do

This won't replace a more complete practice for consistent, ongoing symptom management, but it does provide real relief on the days you have the least capacity, which is often exactly when relief matters most. Building toward a longer routine on better-energy days is a reasonable next step whenever you have more to give.
