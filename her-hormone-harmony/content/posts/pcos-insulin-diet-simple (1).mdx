You don't need meal prep Sundays and a color-coded spreadsheet to make progress on PCOS insulin resistance. You need two or three habits that survive a genuinely full week, the kind where dinner sometimes happens standing up over the sink, not the ideal version that only works when life fully cooperates.

Here's the version built for right now, with actual time estimates so you know exactly what you're committing to.

## The one rule that covers the most ground

Never eat a carb completely alone. Toast gets peanut butter. Fruit gets a handful of nuts. Rice gets chicken that's already in the fridge from last night. This single habit, applied consistently, blunts more blood sugar spikes than almost anything else on this list, and it requires no new shopping trip, no meal plan, and no extra prep time beyond opening a second container instead of one.

## The walk that costs ten minutes

A short walk after your largest meal of the day, even around the block, up and down your hallway, or pacing while on a phone call, measurably lowers the blood sugar spike from that meal. It doesn't need to be a workout, and it doesn't need special clothes or a plan. It needs to happen most days, ideally right after eating rather than hours later.

## Breakfast, simplified to one decision

Pick one high-protein breakfast you actually like and eat it on repeat during busy weeks: eggs, Greek yogurt with nuts, or a protein shake with a piece of fruit blended in. Decision fatigue is real, especially on top of everything else on your plate, and removing this one choice from your mornings makes the rest of the day slightly easier to manage too, since one fewer decision in the morning tends to protect willpower for later.

## If you have five minutes versus twenty

Five minutes: the protein breakfast, and a carb-plus-protein pairing at whatever meal is easiest to control, usually lunch if it's something you're assembling yourself. Twenty minutes: add the post-meal walk and a quick bodyweight strength circuit, squats, push-ups, lunges, done at home with zero equipment needed.

## Strength training, minimum viable version

Two fifteen-minute bodyweight sessions a week, squats, push-ups, lunges, done at home with no equipment, beats zero sessions of the hour-long gym routine you don't currently have time for. Muscle built this way still improves insulin sensitivity, just at a slower pace than a more involved program would, which is a completely reasonable tradeoff for a season of life with limited time.

## What to let go of for now

Skip tracking every gram of fiber or calculating glycemic index scores this month, that level of precision is genuinely useful eventually but adds friction right when you need less of it. Consistency on the basics matters more right now than precision on the details. Add complexity later, once these habits stop requiring conscious effort and start happening automatically.

## What this version realistically won't do

This stripped-down approach moves slower than a fuller program with more structure, since you're doing fewer things overall. That's an honest tradeoff, not a flaw, and it's worth knowing upfront so slower progress doesn't feel like failure. The goal here is sustainable movement in the right direction, not the fastest possible results.

## Checking in later

Give this simplified version six to eight weeks before judging it. If energy and cravings are improving even slightly, even in ways that feel small day to day, you're on the right track and can consider adding one more layer, more structured meal timing, actual meal prep, once capacity opens up in your schedule.
