Structuring an entire training block around your cycle, mapping out intensity waves, adjusting volume week to week, is genuinely useful and also more planning than a lot of real weeks have room for. If the full periodization approach feels like too much, here's the version that captures most of the benefit with one simple adjustment.

## The one decision that covers the most ground

In the one to two weeks after your period ends, push your hardest set or your fastest interval a little further than you normally would, more weight, one more rep, a bit more pace. That's the entire adjustment. You don't need a mapped-out program, just permission to push slightly harder during this specific window when your body has more available.

## What this looks like in a normal workout

If you're already doing a strength session, add weight to your last set of your main lift instead of repeating the same weight from last time. If you're doing cardio, push your pace on your final interval or your last mile a bit harder than usual. This is a small, low-effort addition to a workout you were already doing anyway.

## What to skip for now

Detailed periodization, tracking every session's volume and intensity, and mapping the whole month in advance can all wait. The single "push a little harder this week" adjustment captures the core idea without requiring a program to maintain.

## What if you're not sure exactly when your follicular phase is

A rough estimate is enough here. If your period ended sometime in the last one to two weeks, you're most likely still in this window, and precision beyond that isn't necessary for this simplified version to work. Waiting for perfect certainty before pushing a little harder just delays a benefit you could otherwise be using.

## Why this one small habit is worth keeping even if you never add more

Consistently pushing a little harder during the same recurring window each month adds up over a year even without any other structure layered on top. This single habit, repeated across many cycles, can meaningfully contribute to progress over time, which means it's worth keeping exactly as simple as it is if that's what allows it to actually happen every month.

## How to know if this window is happening for you right now

If it's been about a week since your period ended, you're likely in this window. You don't need an app or exact tracking, a rough sense of timing is enough for this simplified version to work.

## What to do the rest of the month

Nothing different is required. Continue your normal training the other three weeks, and simply notice, without needing to formally adjust anything, that this specific window might feel like the natural place to push a bit further if your body has the capacity for it that particular cycle.

## Building on this later

Once pushing a little harder during this window feels automatic, that's a natural point to consider adding more structure, actual progressive overload tracking, or easing back more deliberately during your luteal phase, if you have the bandwidth to build it out further.
