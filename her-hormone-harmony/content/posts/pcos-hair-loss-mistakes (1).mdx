You've tried the spearmint tea. Maybe the biotin gummies too. And your hairline looks exactly the same as it did three months ago, which is genuinely discouraging when you did the thing everyone recommended and still ended up back at the mirror, checking the same spot.

Most of the time, the remedy wasn't wrong. The execution was. Here's where it usually goes sideways, in the order that tends to matter most.

## Mistake one: expecting results in weeks

Hair follicles cycle slowly, moving through a growth phase, a short transition phase, and a resting phase that can last months. A follicle that's resting when you start spearmint tea won't respond to it until it re-enters growth, which might not happen for eight or ten weeks on its own timeline, not yours. Quitting at six weeks because "nothing changed" means quitting before the biology had any real chance to show up. Give any single intervention sixteen weeks minimum, ideally with monthly photos, before judging whether it worked.

## Mistake two: treating biotin as the fix

Biotin is the most recommended, least useful supplement for PCOS hair loss specifically. Unless you have an actual deficiency, which is uncommon outside of certain medical conditions or medications, extra biotin does very little to interrupt androgen-driven follicle damage. It's not harmful, and it won't hurt to take it, it's just not targeting the actual mechanism, which is DHT overproduction, not a vitamin shortage. Marketing has done a lot of work convincing women otherwise, mostly because biotin is cheap to produce and easy to sell as a general "hair health" ingredient.

## Mistake three: fixing the diet but skipping the insulin piece

Cutting sugar helps, but if you're still eating large refined-carb meals that spike blood sugar hard, insulin stays elevated enough to keep pushing androgen production up regardless. Pairing carbs with protein and fat, and choosing whole grains most of the time, matters as much as cutting sugar outright, since both target the same insulin pathway from different angles. A lot of women do half of this equation, cut the obvious sugar, skip the carb-pairing habit, and end up confused about why symptoms haven't moved much.

## Mistake four: only addressing the scalp, not the whole pattern

Minoxidil can slow scalp thinning, but it does nothing for the facial hair growth happening from the same androgen excess. Treating one symptom without addressing the shared cause means managing half the problem while the other half continues untouched, and it can feel like you're fighting two separate battles when really it's one battle showing up in two places. The food and lifestyle changes matter precisely because they address both symptoms at their shared root, rather than patching each one separately with a different product.

## Mistake five: stacking five new things at once, then stopping all of them

It's tempting to start spearmint tea, cut dairy, add zinc, start strength training, and try a new supplement all in the same week, especially when you're motivated and want results fast. When nothing feels different a month later, most people abandon everything at once, without ever knowing which piece, if any, was starting to help. Add one or two changes at a time, give each a real four-to-six-week trial, and only then decide whether to add the next layer. This is slower to start but far more likely to actually stick.

## A sixth pattern worth naming: comparing your timeline to someone else's

Every body responds differently based on how severe the underlying androgen excess is, how sensitive your specific follicles are to DHT, and how consistent you've genuinely been. Watching someone else's transformation photos on social media, without knowing their starting point or what else they changed, sets an unfair benchmark for your own progress. Your comparison point should be your own photos from a month ago, not a stranger's highlight reel.

## What actually moves the needle

Consistency on two or three well-chosen changes for four to six months beats a scattered five-thing protocol abandoned after five weeks. This isn't a quick fix, and anyone framing it as one is setting you up for exactly the discouragement you're probably already feeling.
