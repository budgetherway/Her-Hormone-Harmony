A ten-step skincare routine and a complete overhaul of your diet is a lot to take on while you're already dealing with acne that's frustrating enough on its own. Here's the simplified version that addresses the two biggest levers without demanding a full routine.

## The one skincare change worth making

Switch to a gentle, non-comedogenic cleanser and moisturizer if you're not already using one, and stop there for now. This single change removes one of the most common ways acne treatment backfires, over-drying skin with harsh products, without requiring you to research or purchase an entire new routine.

## The one food habit worth building

Pair carbohydrates with protein and fat at meals, especially breakfast, rather than eating them alone. This single habit addresses the insulin-androgen connection relevant to hormonal acne without requiring you to eliminate any specific food group or plan detailed meals around it.

## If you can add one more thing

A basic salicylic acid spot treatment, used only on active breakouts rather than the whole face, addresses surface congestion without the broader irritation a full-face treatment can cause. This is a reasonable second step once the gentler routine feels established.

## What to skip for now

Retinoids, prescription options, and detailed elimination diets can wait. They're genuinely useful tools, and they're also things that benefit from more bandwidth to use correctly and consistently than a stripped-down starting approach requires.

## What to do about makeup during a flare

Choosing non-comedogenic makeup, and washing brushes or sponges more regularly than you might otherwise think to, prevents makeup from compounding a flare that's already happening for hormonal reasons. This doesn't require switching every product you own, just checking the labels on what touches your skin most directly.

## A note on patience with this simplified version

Two changes held consistently for months will outperform a complicated ten-step routine abandoned after two weeks, and that's true whether you're managing hormonal acne or almost anything else this site talks about. The simplified version isn't a compromise, it's a legitimate strategy for a season where more isn't realistic.

## What to expect from this simplified version

Slower improvement than a fully comprehensive approach, since fewer levers are being pulled at once, but real improvement over three to four months is still realistic with just these two changes held consistently. This isn't a lesser version of skincare, it's the version most likely to actually happen consistently during a season with limited bandwidth.

## When to consider more support

If acne is severe, cystic, or shows no change after four months of these basics, that's worth bringing to a dermatologist rather than continuing to wait, regardless of how simplified your approach has been. A specific medical option like spironolactone might be exactly what a more complex hormonal pattern actually needs.
