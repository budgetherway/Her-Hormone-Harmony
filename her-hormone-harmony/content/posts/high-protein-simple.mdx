Calculating protein grams by phase, planning meals around it, prepping ahead so the numbers actually hit, all of that assumes a level of kitchen time and planning that a lot of real weeks simply don't have available. If that's kept you from trying this at all, here's the version built around what you can grab, not what you have to cook.

## The one number worth remembering

During the week before your period, aim to add one extra protein source to your usual day, an extra egg at breakfast, a scoop of protein powder in your coffee, a handful of edamame as a snack. You don't need to calculate exact grams, you need one meaningful addition during the window your body needs it most.

## Grab-and-go options that require zero cooking

Greek yogurt cups, hard-boiled eggs made once and kept in the fridge for the week, rotisserie chicken from the store, canned tuna, and pre-cooked shrimp all deliver real protein with essentially no prep time beyond opening a container. Keeping two or three of these stocked covers most of a week without a single recipe involved.

## The five-minute breakfast that changes the whole day

Greek yogurt with a scoop of protein powder or a handful of nuts stirred in takes less time than making toast and delivers twenty-five or more grams of protein before you've left the house. Starting the day this way, especially during the luteal phase, reduces how hard cravings hit later without requiring any advance planning the night before.

## If cooking happens even once a week

Cooking a large batch of chicken, ground turkey, or tofu on whichever day you do have twenty minutes gives you a protein source ready to add to whatever else you're already eating for the rest of the week, salads, rice bowls, wraps, without needing to cook protein again until the batch runs out.

## What to do about restaurant and takeout days

Ordering with protein in mind, adding a side of grilled chicken or an extra egg to whatever you're already getting, keeps this approach working even on days you don't cook at all. Most restaurants and fast casual spots have some way to add protein for a small cost, and asking for it takes the same five seconds as any other modification to an order.

## A note on snacking during the harder week

Keeping a protein-forward snack within reach, string cheese, a hard-boiled egg, a small container of cottage cheese, changes what you reach for automatically when a craving hits during the luteal phase, without requiring willpower in the moment to choose it deliberately over whatever's closest.

## What to skip for now

Precise gram tracking and phase-specific meal plans can wait until this feels automatic. The goal right now is simply eating more protein than you currently do, especially in the week before your period, not hitting an exact number calculated to the gram.

## What to expect from this simplified version

Less precise than a fully mapped plan, but still meaningfully different from doing nothing, since even one consistent extra protein source during your luteal phase addresses a real part of the hunger and muscle loss pattern this approach targets. Build toward more structure only once grabbing that extra protein source feels like second nature rather than one more thing to remember.
