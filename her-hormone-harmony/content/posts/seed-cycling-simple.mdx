Remembering to switch between two different seed pairs at the right point in your cycle, every single month, is exactly the kind of small habit that quietly falls apart after a couple of weeks. If that's happened to you already, here's the version built to actually stick.

## The one prep step that makes this effortless

Buy both seed pairs at the same time and pre-portion a week's worth of each into small containers or bags right away. When it's time to switch, you're grabbing a ready container instead of measuring anything out, which removes the exact friction point where this habit usually breaks down.

## Tying this to something you already do daily

Keep whichever seed pair is current sitting right next to your coffee maker, your cereal, or wherever else you already have a consistent morning habit. Attaching the seed habit to an existing routine means you don't need to remember it as a separate task floating on its own.

## The simplest possible switching system

Since the seed switch happens roughly twice a cycle, around ovulation and around your period, tying it to markers you're likely already aware of removes the need for a separate reminder system. A rough sense of timing is completely adequate here, precision to the exact day isn't necessary for this practice to work as intended.

## What to skip for now

Precise gram measurements, tracking exact intake daily, and researching every nutrient in depth can all wait. One tablespoon each, added to something you're already eating, covers the practical core of the habit without needing more precision than that.

## What if you forget to switch on the exact right day

Missing the exact switch day by a day or two either direction makes essentially no meaningful difference, since this isn't a precisely time-sensitive medical intervention. Aiming for roughly the right half of your cycle is more than sufficient, and stressing over exact timing adds pressure without adding any real benefit.

## If even two seed pairs feels like too much to manage

Picking just one pair, flaxseed and pumpkin during the first half of your cycle, and skipping the switch entirely still provides real nutritional benefit from lignans and zinc, even without the full four-seed rotation. A partial version consistently maintained beats a complete version abandoned after a few weeks.

## What this simplified version can and can't do

This captures the practical nutritional benefit of the individual seeds without requiring precise tracking or elaborate planning. Given that the complete practice's evidence base is modest to begin with, a simplified, consistently maintained version is a completely reasonable way to engage with it, not a lesser version of "doing it properly."
