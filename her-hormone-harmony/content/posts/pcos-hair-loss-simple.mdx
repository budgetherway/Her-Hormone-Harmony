You don't need to overhaul your kitchen, your supplement cabinet, and your workout schedule in the same week to make progress on PCOS hair loss. You need two or three things done consistently, not ten things done for five days and then quietly abandoned when the week gets busy again.

Here's the version built for a week that's already full, broken down by how much time you actually have on a given day.

## The one habit that requires zero extra time

Brew spearmint tea in the morning while you're making coffee anyway, and again in the afternoon instead of a second coffee. It replaces something you're already doing rather than adding a new task to your day, which is the whole reason it survives busy weeks when other habits don't. This alone, done consistently for a few months, is one of the better-evidenced pieces of this entire approach, and it costs you nothing but a tea bag and five minutes of steeping time.

## The five-minute food swap

Keep a bag of pumpkin seeds wherever you already snack, your desk drawer, your bag, the kitchen counter next to where you grab things on the way out. A small handful in place of chips or crackers gets you meaningful zinc without a single extra minute of meal prep or a grocery list change beyond one item.

## If you can only change one meal

Pick breakfast. Swap white toast or a plain bagel for something with protein attached, eggs, Greek yogurt with nuts, oatmeal with a scoop of protein powder stirred in. Blood sugar spikes hardest after a carb-only breakfast eaten on an empty stomach, so fixing this one meal does more for the whole day than scattered changes spread thin across breakfast, lunch, and dinner.

## If you have five minutes versus fifteen

Five minutes: the tea, the pumpkin seed handful, and a protein-forward breakfast, no cooking required beyond boiling water or grabbing yogurt from the fridge. Fifteen minutes: add a bodyweight strength circuit, squats, push-ups against a counter, lunges, done in your living room with zero equipment. You don't need a gym membership or a block of free time you don't have to get real benefit here.

## Movement, minimized

Two twenty-minute strength sessions a week beats zero sessions of the "proper" hour-long routine you keep meaning to start but never actually begin. Bodyweight squats, push-ups, and a resistance band at home cover enough ground to matter for insulin sensitivity, which indirectly affects androgen production. This isn't the ideal version of a fitness routine, it's the version you'll actually do, and a version you do consistently beats an ideal version you do twice.

## What to skip for now

Don't worry about supplements beyond what's already in your food right now. Saw palmetto, extra zinc capsules, and other additions are worth exploring once the basics are consistent, not before you've even tested whether the free changes make a difference. Adding five things at once when you're already stretched thin usually means abandoning all five within a month, and you'll have no idea which one might have actually helped.

## What this version can't do

A stripped-down protocol moves slower than the full version, since you're doing less of it overall. That's a fair tradeoff for something you'll actually sustain, but it's worth being honest about: if progress feels too slow after a few months, that's the moment to layer in one more piece, not a sign that this approach doesn't work for you.

## The realistic timeline for this version

Slower than the full protocol, but slower and sustained beats faster and abandoned every time hair growth is involved, since hair simply doesn't respond on a rushed schedule regardless of how motivated you are. Revisit this in two months and see what capacity you have to add one more piece, rather than trying to do everything on day one and burning out by week three.
