Every recipe here follows the same two rules that matter most for PCOS: pair every carbohydrate with protein and fat, and hit real fiber numbers without it feeling like a chore. Neither rule requires unusual ingredients, just a specific way of combining ordinary ones.

## Cinnamon Baked Oatmeal with Walnuts

Cinnamon has modest evidence for supporting insulin sensitivity, and walnuts add protein and fat that slow how quickly the oats affect blood sugar compared to a bowl of plain oatmeal.

**Ingredients:** 2 cups rolled oats, 2 eggs, 1 cup milk, 1 tsp cinnamon, 1/2 cup walnuts, 1 tbsp honey, 1 tsp baking powder.

**Instructions:** Mix all ingredients, pour into a greased baking dish, bake at 350°F for 30 minutes until set.

## Lentil and Roasted Vegetable Salad

Lentils bring both protein and fiber in one ingredient, exactly the combination that blunts a blood sugar spike most effectively.

**Ingredients:** 1 cup cooked lentils, 2 cups roasted vegetables (zucchini, bell pepper, red onion), 2 cups arugula, 2 tbsp olive oil, 1 tbsp balsamic vinegar.

**Instructions:** Toss roasted vegetables with lentils and arugula. Whisk olive oil and vinegar, drizzle over the salad before serving.

## Salmon with Cauliflower Rice Stir-Fry

Cauliflower rice keeps this meal lower on the glycemic scale than regular rice, while salmon provides omega-3s that support the anti-inflammatory piece of managing PCOS.

**Ingredients:** 2 salmon fillets, 3 cups riced cauliflower, 1 cup mixed vegetables (peas, carrots, edamame), 2 tbsp soy sauce, 1 egg, sesame oil.

**Instructions:** Pan-sear salmon, set aside. In the same pan, scramble egg, add cauliflower rice and vegetables, stir-fry 5 minutes, add soy sauce. Serve with flaked salmon on top.

## Greek Yogurt Chia Pudding with Berries

Chia seeds add significant fiber to a protein-rich base, and berries provide natural sweetness with a lower glycemic impact than most fruit.

**Ingredients:** 1 cup Greek yogurt, 2 tbsp chia seeds, 1/2 cup mixed berries, 1 tsp vanilla, cinnamon to taste.

**Instructions:** Stir chia seeds and vanilla into yogurt, refrigerate at least 2 hours or overnight. Top with berries and a dash of cinnamon before serving.

## Chickpea and Spinach Curry

A fiber-dense, protein-forward curry that satisfies a craving for something warm and filling without the blood sugar spike a rice-heavy takeout version might bring.

**Ingredients:** 1 can chickpeas, 3 cups spinach, 1 can coconut milk, 1 tbsp curry powder, 1 onion, garlic, ginger.

**Instructions:** Sauté onion, garlic, and ginger, add curry powder, cook 1 minute. Add chickpeas and coconut milk, simmer 10 minutes, stir in spinach until wilted.

## Making these work for your kitchen

Any firm white fish or chicken breast substitutes for salmon in the stir-fry, and canned lentils work fine in place of cooking them from dry if time is tight. Coconut yogurt makes the chia pudding dairy-free without losing the protein and fiber combination that makes it work.

## Building these into a full week

The lentil salad and curry both hold up well for several days in the fridge, making them strong candidates for a larger batch made once and eaten across multiple meals. The chia pudding is worth making the night before consistently, since it needs several hours to set and becomes an easy grab-and-go breakfast once that habit is established.

## Why these recipes work together

Every single one pairs carbohydrate with protein and fat, hits meaningful fiber content, and avoids the refined, low-fiber combinations that spike insulin hardest. This is the actual mechanism behind PCOS-friendly eating, not a specific list of "good" and "bad" foods, just consistent pairing done well.
