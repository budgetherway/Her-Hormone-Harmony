The days you're actually bleeding call for a different kind of meal than the rest of the month: something that replenishes iron, eases inflammation behind cramps, and doesn't ask much of a digestive system that's often running slower than usual this week. These five recipes are built around that, simple enough to make on a low-energy day.

## Ginger Turmeric Lentil Soup

Ginger and turmeric both have real anti-inflammatory properties relevant to period pain, and lentils provide iron to help offset what's being lost through bleeding.

**Ingredients:** 1 cup red lentils, 1 tbsp fresh ginger (grated), 1 tsp turmeric, 1 onion (diced), 4 cups vegetable broth, coconut milk to finish.

**Instructions:** Sauté onion, ginger, and turmeric until fragrant. Add lentils and broth, simmer 20 minutes until lentils are soft. Stir in a splash of coconut milk before serving.

## Spinach and Beef Stir-Fry

Beef provides highly absorbable iron, exactly what's needed during blood loss, and spinach adds even more alongside vitamin C-rich vegetables that help absorption.

**Ingredients:** 8 oz beef strips, 3 cups spinach, 1 bell pepper (sliced), 2 tbsp soy sauce, 1 tbsp sesame oil, garlic, ginger.

**Instructions:** Sear beef in sesame oil until browned, remove. Sauté garlic, ginger, and bell pepper, add spinach until wilted, return beef, toss with soy sauce.

## Dark Chocolate and Magnesium Trail Mix

Magnesium helps relax uterine muscle and ease cramping, and this no-cook mix is easy to have ready on a day cooking feels like too much.

**Ingredients:** 1/4 cup pumpkin seeds, 1/4 cup almonds, 1/4 cup dark chocolate chunks, 2 tbsp dried cherries.

**Instructions:** Combine all ingredients in a jar or bag. No cooking required, just mix and store for easy snacking.

## Warm Ginger Tea with Honey

A simple, research-supported option for cramp relief, ginger tea is worth having on hand specifically for the first day or two when cramps tend to hit hardest.

**Ingredients:** 1 inch fresh ginger (sliced), 2 cups water, honey to taste, lemon slice.

**Instructions:** Simmer ginger in water for 10 minutes. Strain, add honey and a slice of lemon.

## Salmon and Sweet Potato Sheet Pan Dinner

An easy, mostly hands-off dinner combining omega-3s, which help reduce inflammatory prostaglandins behind cramps, with iron-supporting sweet potato and greens.

**Ingredients:** 2 salmon fillets, 2 cups cubed sweet potato, 2 cups broccoli, olive oil, salt, pepper, garlic powder.

**Instructions:** Toss sweet potato with oil and seasoning, roast at 400°F for 15 minutes. Add salmon and broccoli to the pan, roast another 12 to 15 minutes until salmon is cooked through.

## Making these work for your kitchen

Chicken thighs work in place of beef for the stir-fry if red meat isn't something you eat regularly, with a vitamin C-rich side to support iron absorption either way. Any white fish substitutes for salmon in the sheet pan dinner.

## How to prepare for this week ahead of time

Since energy for cooking is often lowest during this phase, making the lentil soup a day or two before your period starts, when you can anticipate it roughly from your tracking, means a comforting, ready meal is already waiting rather than something to manage on a low-energy day. The trail mix and ginger tea ingredients are both worth keeping stocked continuously, since they need no advance prep at all.

## Why these recipes work together

Iron replaces what bleeding takes, magnesium and ginger address inflammation and cramping directly, and every recipe here is simple enough to make on a day when standing at the stove for long feels like more than you have energy for. This week isn't the time for elaborate cooking, and none of these ask for it.
