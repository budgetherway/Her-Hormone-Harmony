Mapping out a full week-by-week training intensity plan across your whole cycle is genuinely useful, and also genuinely more than most weeks have room for. If the detailed version feels like too much to manage right now, here's the simplified take that still addresses the core problem.

## The one swap that covers the most ground

During the week before your period, replace your hardest scheduled workout that week, whatever it normally is, with a walk or a gentle yoga session instead. You don't need to redesign your whole training split, just soften the single most demanding session during the window your body is least equipped to recover from it well.

## How to know which workout to swap

Whichever session usually leaves you the most wrecked afterward on a normal week, that's the one to swap during your luteal phase specifically. This is more useful than trying to categorize every workout by intensity level, since you already intuitively know which one hits hardest.

## If you only have energy to change one more thing

Move your bedtime up by thirty minutes during the same week. Progesterone's effect on body temperature can disrupt sleep quality during this window, and a little extra time in bed partially compensates for sleep that might be lighter or more fragmented than usual, supporting recovery without requiring any change to your workouts themselves.

## What to do if you don't know your cycle length exactly

Estimate a week to ten days before your period based on your last cycle or two, rather than waiting for a perfectly precise prediction before making any adjustment. Being roughly right and consistent about softening one workout beats waiting for exact certainty that delays taking any action at all.

## A simple way to remember which workout to swap

Write the swap into whatever calendar or app you already use to track your period, the same one place you're already checking. Pairing the reminder with something you're checking anyway removes the need to remember a separate system just for this one adjustment.

## What to skip for now

Detailed intensity percentages, a fully mapped training calendar, and precise cortisol-focused recovery protocols can all wait. The single swapped workout and slightly earlier bedtime cover the two highest-impact changes without asking for a system to maintain.

## What this simplified version won't fully solve

If exhaustion and weight retention during this window are severe, this lighter version may not fully resolve the pattern the way a more complete approach would. It's a reasonable starting point that reduces the problem meaningfully without requiring a full training overhaul, and it's worth building on with more structure later if you have the capacity for it.

## When to consider adding more

Once swapping one workout and adjusting bedtime feels automatic, usually after a couple of cycles, that's a natural point to consider softening a second session or looking more closely at sleep environment changes, layering in more structure only once the basics feel effortless rather than like one more thing to manage.
