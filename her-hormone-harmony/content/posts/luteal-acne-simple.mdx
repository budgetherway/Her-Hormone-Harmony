Reformulating your whole diet around omega-3s and switching your entire skincare routine to anti-inflammatory ingredients is a lot to take on for a breakout pattern that shows up for less than two weeks a month. Here's the simplified version that addresses the core mechanism with minimal effort.

## The one food habit worth building

Add a serving of fatty fish, or a tablespoon of ground flaxseed or chia seeds if fish isn't your thing, two to three times during the week before your period specifically. This single habit addresses the omega-3 piece without requiring you to overhaul your regular eating.

## The one skincare swap worth making

Switch to a gentle, non-drying cleanser and add a niacinamide serum if you don't already use one, keeping the rest of your routine as is. This single swap addresses the anti-inflammatory piece without requiring a full routine change.

## What to do instead of picking

Keep a hydrocolloid pimple patch on hand for the couple of breakouts that do show up, giving your hands something else to do besides picking at a painful spot. This is a small, concrete substitute that protects against the scarring risk this specific acne type carries.

## What if you don't like fish or flax

Chia seeds are an easy alternative, easy to stir into yogurt or oatmeal without much taste, and a basic omega-3 supplement is a reasonable fallback if none of the food sources appeal to you regularly. The goal is consistent omega-3 intake in whatever form you'll actually keep up, not a specific food you feel obligated to eat.

## What to skip for now

Detailed food tracking, a full skincare overhaul, and precise cycle-based product switching can all wait. The fish or flax habit and the gentle cleanser swap cover the highest-impact pieces without asking for more effort than you have available.

## What this simplified version can and can't do

This won't eliminate the pattern entirely, since it's tied to a real hormonal shift that happens regardless, but it does meaningfully reduce severity without requiring a full routine overhaul. If breakouts remain severe despite this simplified approach, building out more support later, or a dermatologist conversation, makes sense once you have more bandwidth.
