Rising progesterone in the luteal phase brings real cravings, usually for something sweet, salty, or carb-heavy, along with lower energy and, for a lot of women, digestion that feels sluggish rather than smooth. These five recipes are built to satisfy that craving pattern honestly, rather than fighting it, while still supporting steadier blood sugar and energy.

None of these are "diet" versions of comfort food that leave you unsatisfied. The goal is food that actually feels like enough during a week when appetite is genuinely higher than usual.

## Sweet Potato and Black Bean Chili

A warm, filling meal that satisfies the pull toward heartier food, with fiber and protein that help slow the blood sugar spike a craving-driven meal might otherwise cause.

**Ingredients:** 2 cups diced sweet potato, 1 can black beans, 1 can diced tomatoes, 1 onion (diced), 2 tbsp chili powder, 1 tbsp cumin, vegetable broth.

**Instructions:** Sauté onion until soft, add sweet potato, beans, tomatoes, and spices. Add enough broth to cover, simmer 25 minutes until sweet potato is tender.

## Dark Chocolate Avocado Mousse

Real dark chocolate satisfies the specific craving progesterone tends to bring, while avocado adds healthy fat and magnesium instead of just sugar alone.

**Ingredients:** 2 ripe avocados, 1/4 cup cocoa powder, 3 tbsp maple syrup, 1/4 cup milk of choice, pinch of salt.

**Instructions:** Blend all ingredients until completely smooth. Chill at least 30 minutes before serving.

## Salty Roasted Chickpeas

For the salty craving specifically, roasted chickpeas provide a satisfying crunch along with fiber and protein that a bag of chips won't.

**Ingredients:** 1 can chickpeas (drained and dried), 1 tbsp olive oil, 1 tsp salt, 1/2 tsp smoked paprika, 1/2 tsp garlic powder.

**Instructions:** Toss chickpeas with oil and seasonings, roast at 400°F for 25 to 30 minutes, shaking halfway, until crisp.

## Magnesium-Rich Spinach and Salmon Pasta

Progesterone can worsen magnesium depletion, and this pasta pairs spinach with salmon for a meal that's genuinely comforting while restocking a nutrient this phase burns through faster.

**Ingredients:** 8 oz whole wheat pasta, 2 salmon fillets, 3 cups spinach, 2 cloves garlic, 2 tbsp olive oil, parmesan, lemon juice.

**Instructions:** Cook pasta according to package directions. Pan-sear salmon, flake into pieces. Sauté garlic and spinach in olive oil until wilted, toss with pasta, salmon, parmesan, and a squeeze of lemon.

## Peanut Butter Banana Oat Bars

A grab-and-go option for a low-energy morning, combining oats, banana, and peanut butter for sustained energy rather than a quick spike and crash.

**Ingredients:** 2 cups oats, 2 ripe bananas (mashed), 1/2 cup peanut butter, 1/4 cup honey, 1/4 cup dark chocolate chips.

**Instructions:** Mix all ingredients until combined, press into a lined baking dish, bake at 350°F for 20 minutes. Cool before cutting into bars.

## Making these work for your kitchen

Any nut butter swaps in for peanut butter in the oat bars, and white beans work in place of black beans in the chili if that's what's in the pantry. A dairy-free chocolate works fine in the mousse for anyone avoiding dairy.

## How to use these across the week

Making a double batch of the chili and freezing half means a comforting, craving-appropriate meal is ready without cooking again mid-week, exactly when energy for cooking tends to be lowest. Keeping the roasted chickpeas and energy bites made ahead means the salty and sweet cravings both have a ready answer before a less balanced option gets reached for instead.

## Why these recipes work together

Each one addresses the actual craving, sweet, salty, or heavy, while adding fiber, protein, or magnesium that a straightforwardly indulgent version wouldn't include. This isn't about resisting the craving, it's about meeting it in a way that also supports the blood sugar stability this phase specifically needs.
